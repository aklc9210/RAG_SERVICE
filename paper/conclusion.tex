\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several limitations should be noted. First, the ingredient hierarchy is constructed via LLM classification with manual validation of only the top 150 ingredients; tail-class accuracy is unverified, and the fallback \texttt{Other} class (427 ingredients) may contain misclassifications. Second, the LLM-judge ground truth for Task~3 achieves only fair inter-annotator agreement ($\kappa = 0.184$), reflecting systematic differences in scoring behavior across models. Third, the ontology is monolingual (Vietnamese) and has not been tested in cross-lingual settings. Fourth, the dish taxonomy uses only two axes (type and method); additional axes such as regional origin, nutritional profile, or occasion could improve similarity modeling. Finally, the substitution scoring function (Eq.~\ref{eq:sub_score}) uses fixed bonus weights ($\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$) that were not tuned on a held-out set.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented an ontology-enhanced RAG framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We constructed a four-level ingredient hierarchy covering 8{,}112 ingredients across 49 classes, a two-axis dish taxonomy, and seven named relations derived from a 10{,}741-dish corpus. The ontology was integrated into a dense-retrieval pipeline at three injection points: query expansion, constrained substitution reasoning, and hierarchy-aware dish similarity.

Evaluation on three tasks showed that ontology structure provides consistent gains over both lexical and dense baselines. For class-based retrieval, RAG+Ontology improved NDCG@20 by +37\% over RAG-only, with hierarchy expansion contributing more than flat synonym lookup. For related-dish recommendation, the four-component similarity formula achieved Spearman $\rho = 0.70$ against human-judged relatedness, with ontology-derived components accounting for half the similarity signal. Ingredient substitution showed more modest improvement (+8.2\%), indicating that ontology provides necessary but not sufficient structure for this task.

The results demonstrate that structured semantic knowledge, encoded as an ontology with typed relations and class hierarchy, complements neural retrieval in a domain where relevance depends on compositional and categorical reasoning rather than lexical overlap alone.
