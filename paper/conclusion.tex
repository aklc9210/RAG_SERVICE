\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented an ontology-enhanced retrieval framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We constructed a four-level ingredient hierarchy covering 2{,}112 ingredients across 49 classes, a two-axis dish taxonomy, and seven named relations derived from a 10{,}741-dish corpus. The ontology was integrated into a dense-retrieval pipeline at two injection points: query expansion and hierarchy-aware dish similarity.

Evaluation on two tasks showed that ontology structure provides consistent gains over both lexical and dense baselines. For class-based retrieval, Dense+Ontology improved NDCG@20 by +37\% and MRR@20 by +39\% over Dense, with hierarchy expansion contributing more than flat synonym lookup. For related-dish recommendation, a component ablation study with diverse candidates and 5-fold cross-validation identified the independent contribution of each ontology signal, with optimized weights determined by the best-performing configuration.

The results demonstrate that structured semantic knowledge, encoded as an ontology with typed relations and class hierarchy, complements neural retrieval in a domain where relevance depends on compositional and categorical reasoning rather than lexical overlap alone.

\textbf{Limitations.}
The ontology is constructed from a single Vietnamese recipe corpus and may not generalize to other cuisines without re-deriving the class hierarchy and relation set. The LLM-as-judge evaluation, while cost-effective and reproducible, captures only a proxy for human culinary judgment; the moderate human--LLM correlation ($\rho = 0.68$) suggests room for improvement in future annotation efforts.
