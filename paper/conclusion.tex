
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented an ontology-enhanced RAG framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We constructed a four-level ingredient hierarchy covering 8{,}112 ingredients across 49 classes, a two-axis dish taxonomy, and seven named relations derived from a 10{,}741-dish corpus. The ontology was integrated into a dense-retrieval pipeline at three injection points: query expansion, constrained substitution reasoning, and hierarchy-aware dish similarity.

Evaluation on three tasks showed that ontology structure provides consistent gains over both lexical and dense baselines. For class-based retrieval, RAG+Ontology improved NDCG@20 by +37\% over RAG-only, with hierarchy expansion contributing more than flat synonym lookup. For related-dish recommendation, the four-component similarity formula achieved Spearman $\rho = 0.70$ against human-judged relatedness, while the optimized weights assign half of the similarity mass to ontology-informed components, indicating that structured signals play an important role in the ranking function. Ingredient substitution showed more modest improvement (+8.2\% over random\_class), indicating that ontology provides necessary but not sufficient structure for this task.

The results demonstrate that structured semantic knowledge, encoded as an ontology with typed relations and class hierarchy, complements neural retrieval in a domain where relevance depends on compositional and categorical reasoning rather than lexical overlap alone.
