
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented an ontology-enhanced retrieval framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We constructed a four-level ingredient hierarchy covering 2{,}112 ingredients across 49 classes, a two-axis dish taxonomy, and seven named relations derived from a 10{,}741-dish corpus. The ontology was integrated into a dense-retrieval pipeline at three injection points: query expansion, constrained substitution reasoning, and hierarchy-aware dish similarity.

Evaluation on three tasks showed that ontology structure provides consistent gains over both lexical and dense baselines. For class-based retrieval, Dense+Ontology improved NDCG@20 by +37\% and MRR@20 by +39\% over Dense, with hierarchy expansion contributing more than flat synonym lookup. For related-dish recommendation, a component ablation study with diverse candidates and 5-fold cross-validation identified the independent contribution of each ontology signal, with optimized weights determined by the best-performing configuration. Ingredient substitution showed more modest improvement (+8.2\% over random\_class), indicating that ontology provides necessary but not sufficient structure for this task.

The results demonstrate that structured semantic knowledge, encoded as an ontology with typed relations and class hierarchy, complements neural retrieval in a domain where relevance depends on compositional and categorical reasoning rather than lexical overlap alone.
