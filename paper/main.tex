\begin{abstract}
Dense retrieval pipelines rely on learned embeddings, yet in the food domain, relevance often depends on structured culinary knowledge that embeddings alone cannot capture. To address this, we propose an ontology-enhanced framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We construct a comprehensive ontology spanning 10{,}741 dishes, featuring a four-level hierarchy (2{,}112 ingredients, 49 classes) and explicit named relations. This structured knowledge is integrated into a dense pipeline via hierarchical query expansion and a multi-component hierarchy-aware dish similarity function. Evaluation demonstrates substantial gains: for class-based retrieval, our approach improves NDCG@5 by +50\% and MRR@5 by +41\% over dense retrieval alone. Furthermore, an ablation study on related-dish recommendation isolates the independent contributions of class overlap, cooking methods, and flavor complementarity. The results confirm that explicitly structured ontological knowledge effectively complements neural retrieval for compositional and categorical reasoning.
\end{abstract}
