\begin{abstract}
Dense retrieval pipelines rely on learned embeddings for semantic matching, yet in the food domain, relevance often depends on structured culinary knowledge that embeddings alone cannot capture. Class-level queries, constrained ingredient substitution, and hierarchy-aware dish similarity all require reasoning over ingredient categories and typed relations. This paper proposes an ontology-enhanced retrieval framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We construct a four-level ingredient hierarchy (2{,}112 ingredients, 49 classes), a two-axis dish taxonomy, and seven named relations over a corpus of 10{,}741 Vietnamese dishes. The ontology is integrated into a dense-retrieval pipeline at three injection points: query expansion via class descendants, constrained substitution via typed relations and hierarchy filtering, and dish similarity via a four-component scoring function combining Jaccard overlap, class overlap, cooking-method match, and semantic similarity. Evaluation on three tasks shows consistent gains: class-based retrieval improves by +37\% NDCG@20 and +39\% MRR@20 over dense retrieval alone, constrained substitution improves by +8.2\% mean score, and a component ablation on related-dish recommendation with diverse candidates isolates the independent contribution of each ontology signal. The results demonstrate that structured ontological knowledge complements neural retrieval when relevance depends on compositional and categorical reasoning.
\end{abstract}
