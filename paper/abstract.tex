\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines rely on dense retrieval for semantic matching, yet in the food domain, relevance often depends on structured culinary knowledge that embeddings alone cannot capture. Class-level queries, constrained ingredient substitution, and hierarchy-aware dish similarity all require reasoning over ingredient categories and typed relations. This paper proposes an ontology-enhanced RAG framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We construct a four-level ingredient hierarchy (8{,}112 ingredients, 49 classes), a two-axis dish taxonomy, and seven named relations over a corpus of 10{,}741 Vietnamese dishes. The ontology is integrated into a dense-retrieval pipeline at three injection points: query expansion via class descendants, constrained substitution via typed relations and hierarchy filtering, and dish similarity via a four-component scoring function combining Jaccard overlap, class overlap, cooking-method match, and semantic similarity. Evaluation on three tasks shows consistent gains: class-based retrieval improves by +37\% NDCG@20 over RAG-only, related-dish recommendation achieves Spearman $\rho = 0.70$ with ontology components accounting for 50\% of the similarity signal, and constrained substitution improves by +8.2\% mean score. The results demonstrate that structured ontological knowledge complements neural retrieval when relevance depends on compositional and categorical reasoning.
\end{abstract}
