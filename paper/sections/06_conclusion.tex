\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented an ontology-enhanced retrieval framework for Vietnamese food information retrieval. We constructed a four-level ingredient hierarchy covering 2{,}112 ingredients across 49 classes, a two-axis dish taxonomy, and six named relations derived from a 10{,}741-dish corpus. The ontology was integrated into a dense-retrieval pipeline at two injection points: query expansion and hierarchy-aware dish similarity.

Evaluation on two tasks showed that ontology structure provides consistent gains over both lexical and dense baselines. For class-based retrieval, Dense+Ontology improved NDCG@5 by +50\% and MRR@5 by +41\% over Dense, with hierarchy expansion contributing substantially more than flat synonym lookup. For related-dish recommendation, a component ablation study evaluated on a diverse held-out test set identified the independent contribution of each ontology signal: ClassOverlap (+7.4\% P@5), MethodMatch (+2.9\%), and FlavorComplement (+0.7\%), with optimized weights assigning 66\% to ontology-derived components.

The results demonstrate that structured semantic knowledge, encoded as an ontology with typed relations and class hierarchy, complements neural retrieval in a domain where relevance depends on compositional and categorical reasoning rather than lexical overlap alone.
